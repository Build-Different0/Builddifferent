Laws of software evolution

Knowledge is not written. It emerges.

These laws were inferred from repeated behavior across successful, failed, and almost-successful creation journeys.

Law 01

Complexity before trust increases abandonment.

Projects that add operational depth before users understand the core exchange are significantly less likely to reach sustained usage.

91% confidence184K journeys
Law 02

Evaluation must precede autonomy.

AI agents that gain tools before gaining a narrow measurement loop become harder to debug and less likely to ship.

88% confidence96K journeys
Law 03

Identity moves earlier as generation gets faster.

When interfaces appear instantly, successful builders resolve ownership and data structure before expanding visible features.

86% confidence241K journeys
Law 04

Almost-successful products overbuild the second loop.

Many near-breakout products improve secondary workflows before the first value loop becomes repeatable.

84% confidence71K journeys
Law 05

Feedback speed beats planning depth after launch.

Once real usage begins, products with tighter learning loops outperform products with more complete pre-launch plans.

90% confidence318K journeys
Law 06

Every successful project changes the engine.

The knowledge layer is continuously rewritten as new categories, tools, behaviors, and builder species emerge.
